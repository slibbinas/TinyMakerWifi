Resins dialled in for the TinyMaker

Pick a resin, tap once, and your printer is set up for it: exposure, base layers, lift and density, every number printed and measured on this machine.

Why a separate list for this printer? The TinyMaker masks with a colour screen, not a mono one, and a colour screen lets through far less UV - the same resin needs about four times the exposure written on the bottle. Its plate is small as well, 41 × 31 mm, so parts release from the film with different forces. Settings from other printers do not fit. The TinyMaker is an open-source MSLA printer by TinyMaker3D; this page comes from the TinyMakerWifi firmware project.

What the printer does with this

Every resin tested here goes into the printer's resin library. Any TinyMaker owner installs it from the dashboard's Resin card in one tap, edits the numbers afterwards if their own vat asks for it, and gets a Buy link in the same row for the resins that carry a shop address.

The printer lists sixteen named profiles, four of them built in; the ticked one is what the next print uses. Exposure, bottom layers, lift and density travel with the profile, and the line tested by TinyMakerWiFi says which month the numbers were measured.

How these numbers are made

The same sequence, the same machine, written down. That is why two resins here can be compared at all.

One machine, one vat

All of it is printed on the same TinyMaker with the same film and the same UV panel. A different panel or a colder room shifts these numbers, which is exactly why they are measured rather than copied between printers.

The screen is the limit, not the resin

The pixel is 0.1275 mm, so nothing thinner than about two pixels prints in any resin. Features are measured in pixels before they are blamed on chemistry - it has saved a full round on more than one resin.

Written down as it happens

Each step is recorded with its exposure, the resin used, the time it took and what the part looked like afterwards. The published profile is the last line of that record, not a separate opinion.

Density is weighed, not copied

Consumption is worked out from the layer images and the resin's density, so density has to be measured - a known volume on a scale, once per bottle. Until then the card says the number came off the label.

The method keeps moving

When the sequence gains a step, earlier resins go back in the queue. Entries measured before the current method say so on their card instead of passing as finished work.

One pair of hands

Tested and written by Viktoras Šidlauskas, who maintains the TinyMakerWifi firmware. The same hands, the same printer, the same notebook for every resin here.

For resin manufacturers

Send a bottle and it goes through the same sequence. No fee, no agreement, no promise about the outcome: the numbers are published whichever way they come out.

Your product, your name

The resin is listed under the name you use in your own shop, with a link to it. If you would rather see different wording or your own photo on the card, say so and it goes in.

Numbers your customers can use

The result is not a review - it is a working profile. A TinyMaker owner installs it in one tap and prints, instead of burning a tank finding the exposure for a colour screen themselves.

Free to reuse

Quote the figures, link to the page, put them in your own settings table or on the product page. They were measured on one machine and that is stated, so nobody is misled either way.
